# What an object does

Generated by `tools/objops.py --doc`. Do not edit it.

`object_interpreter` (`0x80047010`) is the third call in every frame
and the largest routine in the game. It walks all 396 slots of
`object_table`; for each one it publishes the record at `0x80198394`
and the type's row at `0x80198390`, and switches on **byte +4 of the
record** -- which is a copy of byte +0 of its type's row, the same
class `render_walk` draws on.

Doors, chests, levers, signs and the things that hurt you are one
machine with 236 opcodes.

## Every placed object in the game, by opcode

The type table is **332 rows of 24 bytes in two pieces**. The first
300 are one block on the disc -- `FDAT.T` entry 97 at offset 4 --
and are the same on every level. The other **32 the level brings
with it**: the third block of entry `3n + 1`, 768 bytes, which
`level_load` copies to `0x8019175c`, exactly 7200 bytes past the
base, so they are types 300 to 331. Against a level-0 RAM snapshot
all 332 rows are **7968 of 7968 bytes**.

**Withdrawn: "1424 placed objects have a type of 300 or above,
which is where the table stops, so they have no row and no
opcode".** They have a row, from their own level. It also
explains the boundary `model_of_type` changes its mind at -- the
model is `type + 0x100` below 300 and takes the level from 300 up
because from 300 up the *row* is the level's too. Two facts filed
as a coincidence were one fact.

**0 placed objects are past type 332**, where
reading a row would be reading whatever follows the table.

## And the creatures, which are the same machine again

`actor_tick` (`0x800500a8`) walks the 199 records of
`actor_table`, 0x88 bytes each, and switches on **`actor[+0xe]`**
through a 241-arm table at `0x800128f0`. Thirty of the arms are
behaviours -- opcodes `0x00` to `0x1f` with `0x07` and `0x08`
missing, plus `0x84` and `0xf0` -- and **209 opcodes reach
`0x80052bf8`, which calls `level_hooks + 0x24`**, the same escape
into the level's own code the object machine has at `0x8004b4b4`.

So both machines are built the same way and both hand an opcode
they do not know to the level. That is the game's extension point,
and it is the same one twice.
